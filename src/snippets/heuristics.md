1. Transparency of Structure -- Controls and navigation should give users a sense of overall structure, location, and available actions.
2. Sense of Control and Flexibility -- Users should feel in control, with an interface that works for both novice and experienced users.
3. Responsiveness and Feedback -- User actions should have noticeable effects, with clear communication of status, success, and failure.
4. Predictability -- Users should be able to anticipate the likely outcome of actions via cues and affordances.
5. Error Avoidance and Recovery -- The system should help users avoid errors and recover from them when they occur.
6. Familiarity and Learnability -- Interface elements should conform to real-world or familiar patterns; unfamiliar elements should be well-labeled and memorable.
7. Preference for Function Over Aesthetics -- Elements that could confuse or interfere with a streamlined experience should be avoided.
8. Availability and Visibility of Information -- Information and actions should be easy to find, reducing reliance on memory.
9. Support and Help -- Fallback help should be available when the system alone isn't enough.
10. Consistency and Clarity -- Verbiage, icons, and actions should be standardized and predictable throughout.
11. Fulfillment and Follow Through -- Users should have confidence their requests are being fulfilled over time.
12. Accessibility and Design -- The system should be accessible by assistive technologies, meet contrast guidelines, and feel visually unified.
